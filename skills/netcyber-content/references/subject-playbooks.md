# Subject playbooks

What changes by subject. The topic flow and the contract stay the same everywhere.

## Mathematics

- Every topic: a concrete or real Indian situation first (a mandi, a cricket score, a water tank, a rangoli, a staircase),
  then a picture (number line, area model, graph, construction), then the symbols.
- **Worked examples carry every line**, with the reason for the step in words ("divide both sides by 3, so x is
  alone"). The faded version hides one step. Show the check ("substitute back").
- Diagrams: number lines, bar/area models, coordinate planes with exact scales, constructions step by step (animated
  arcs), solids as nets and as 3D with dashed hidden edges, graphs of the real function (plot points computed, not
  sketched).
- Labs: `number-line`, `fractions`, `algebra`, `grapher`, `shapes`, `solids`, `trig`, `probability`, `data`, `matrix`,
  `pattern` (see `lab-library.md`). Typical new templates worth building: compass-and-ruler construction, similarity
  and scaling, area-by-counting, statistics on a live dataset, AP stairs.
- Distractors from real errors: sign slips, squaring a sum, wrong order of operations, swapping area and perimeter,
  confusing a theorem with its converse, dividing by a variable that may be 0.
- Proofs: state given / to prove / construction / proof with a reason on every line; the faded version blanks one
  reason.

## Science (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, EVS)

- Phenomenon first (rust on a gate, a mirage on a road, curd setting), then the model, then the law/equation.
- Equations in `{{eq:…}}` notation with states; conditions over the arrow; heat given out on the product side.
- Physics: ray diagrams with arrowheads in the direction of travel; current from + to −; field lines N → S outside;
  vectors to scale; units on every line; significant figures as NCERT.
- Biology: structure → function; processes as animated flows (blood, food, impulses, water); organs in context of the
  body; respectful, factual, NCERT-level treatment of reproduction and the body.
- Labs: `beaker`, `particles`, `atom`, `molecule`, `periodic`, `optics`, `circuit`, `field`, `motion`, `forces`,
  `waves`, `heat`, `body`, `plant`, `cross`, `eco`, `scope`, `scatter`, `balance` … Every NCERT activity gets a lab if a
  template can model it honestly; dangerous activities stay virtual with a safety note.

## Social Science (History, Geography, Political Science, Economics)

- Concept through a story, a place or a source: a map, a photograph, a newspaper clipping, a data table (written by
  you or from a cited public dataset).
- Diagrams: timelines, cause → effect chains, maps (`map`, `globe` templates), flow of money and goods, structure of
  government, climate graphs.
- Questions: source-based and map-based items are the "competency" share; avoid one-festival-stands-for-India and
  "development = only cities" (founder framework §5.3.3).
- Labs: `timeline`, `map`, `globe`, `earth`, `economy`, `govern`, `society`, `scenario`, `sort`, `match`, `sequence`,
  `data`. Worth building: map-reading with layers, a mock election, a budget/price simulator.
- Neutral, factual, constitutional language on politics and religion; never take sides.

## Languages (English, Hindi, Sanskrit, Urdu)

- **Never reproduce copyrighted prose or poems.** Teach the skills around the text: comprehension questions on a short
  original summary, vocabulary, grammar, figures of speech, writing forms (letter, notice, report), speaking.
- Diagrams: story maps (character, setting, problem, events, resolution), grammar trees, word families, sentence
  builders.
- Labs: `reader`, `words`, `grammar`, `writing`, `mind`, `match`, `sequence`, `sort`.
- For the Hindi and Sanskrit subjects, the lesson language is that language; the "spoken Hindi" rule applies to the
  explanations, not to the literary text being studied.

## Commerce and Computer Science (Classes 11–12)

- Accountancy: every entry as a T-account and a journal line, balanced; `ledger` template; real Indian small-business
  examples with rupees.
- Business Studies, Economics: cases from Indian businesses (invented, never real company claims); graphs computed.
- Computer Science / IP: runnable examples in the `code` template (no network), trace tables, `logic` and `table`.
- Psychology, Sociology: scenarios (`scenario`, `mind`), research-method cards; sensitive themes with care.

## Foundational and Preparatory (Classes 1–5)

Follow the band table. Short taps, audio first, concrete objects (`place-value`, `fractions`, `money`, `clock`,
`shapes`, `pattern`, `sort`, `match`), no exam language, no hard locks in Class 1.
