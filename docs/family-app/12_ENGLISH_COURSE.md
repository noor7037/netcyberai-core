# 12 — The 90-day English speaking and confidence course

**Status:** decided by the build lead from the founder's brief of 2026-10-22 (C274). It replaces the shape of E2 (the
90-day programme) and brings E4 (the practice partner) into the course as practice only (`11`). Everything else in
`02` §6, `03` and `09` still applies.

**The founder's brief, in short:** a complete English course for children aged 3 to 15 in four age groups, 5 to 10
minutes a day, built for rural Indian families. The course stands on its own content; voice conversation is only for
practice. Lessons open in order. Families get a progress report. The design is colourful and animated, and engaging
without being addictive.

## 1. What the course promises

- **The promise:** in 90 lesson days, a child says more, in better English, with less fear. Progress is measured and
  shown to the parent:
  - the day-0, day-45 and day-90 assessments side by side;
  - the can-do statements achieved ("I can say my name and my class");
  - the words and sentence patterns retained on spaced checks;
  - the time spent speaking.
- **No fluency promise.** A CEFR level takes about 200 guided hours (`01` E16). Ninety days at 5–10 minutes is 8–15
  hours, so each track aims for a measured step, never "fluent in 90 days".
- **Learning first (`09` §1).** A day's lesson ends with something learned and checked. The app never asks for more
  time than the day's lesson, and it has no endless feed.

## 2. The four tracks

| Track | Ages (classes) | Level (CEFR) | Minutes a day | Who is with the child | Reading needed | Hindi support |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Little Speakers** | 3–5 (Nursery to UKG) | Pre-A1, listening and speaking only | 5 | A grown-up starts the lesson and stays | None: pictures and sound | Full: every instruction is spoken and shown in Hindi |
| **B — Young Speakers** | 6–8 (Classes 1–3) | Pre-A1 to A1 | 5–7 | A grown-up nearby; the child taps | Words and short sentences, always with sound | Full, fading in units 13–18 |
| **C — Confident Speakers** | 9–12 (Classes 4–7) | A1 to A2 | 7–8 | The child alone; the parent sees the report | Short stories and dialogues | Meanings and tips; instructions in English from unit 7 |
| **D — Bold Speakers** | 13–15 (Classes 8–10) | A2 to B1 | 8–10 | The child alone | Dialogues, short talks, messages | On request ("Hindi mein samjhao") |

- **Placement:** the track follows the child's age, from their class (Nursery to UKG is A; Classes 1–3 B; 4–7 C;
  8–10 D). The parent may move a child to the track below or above once, from the parent area. Classes 11–12 take
  track D.
- **Starting point:** track A starts at unit 1. In tracks B–D, the day-0 check may place a child at unit 1, 4 or 7.
  The units before are then marked "placed" and stay open to revise.

## 3. The shape of the course

- **18 units of 5 lessons: 90 lesson days.** A unit is a theme from the child's life (family, school, market, fields,
  health, festivals, travel). Its five lessons always follow the same rhythm, so the child knows what comes next:

| Lesson | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Words** | 4–8 new words with pictures, sound and their Hindi meaning; listen, say, match |
| 2 | **Pattern** | One sentence pattern or grammar point, shown with an animated visual; build sentences with tiles |
| 3 | **Listen** | A short story or dialogue in the unit's world; listen, follow the highlighted words, answer |
| 4 | **Speak** | A speaking task: role-play, picture description, question and answer, a talk, or the Hindi-to-English bridge. In duet mode a grown-up takes one part of the role-play |
| 5 | **Review** | Mixed practice of the unit and of older units that are due; the unit check; the can-do statement |

- **Assessments:** day 0 (before unit 1), day 45 (after unit 9) and day 90 (after unit 18). Each has the same
  blueprint and different items, so the three results can be compared.
- **Lesson days, not calendar days.** A missed day changes nothing: the next lesson waits. A weekly goal (for example
  5 lessons in 7 days, set by the parent) replaces streaks; the app never says "you broke your streak" (`11` R7).
- **At most two new lessons a day** (a Control setting). Review is open any time. This keeps the daily habit short
  and spaced, which helps memory more than one long session (spacing, §5).

## 4. A lesson, step by step

Every lesson is a short sequence of steps on full-screen cards, with a progress bar at the top. Each step has a
"listen" button. Nothing needs typing in tracks A and B.

| Step | What the child does | A | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 2–3 items due for review (spaced repetition, §7) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Learn | The lesson's new content: word cards, a pattern visual, a story or a task brief | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Do | Tap, match, order or build: picture choice, word tiles, true or false, gap fill | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Say | Say it aloud: repeat, answer, describe, play a part. Record and hear yourself; voice feedback when it is switched on (§9) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Check | 3–6 items; the lesson is done at 80% or more, after any number of tries | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Done | The can-do line, words collected, and what opens next | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

- **Track A has no scored check:** the grown-up taps "we did it" after the "Say" step, and the "Do" step's picture
  choices are the check. A 3-year-old is never marked wrong.
- **Correction without shame:**
  - a wrong tap shows the right answer with its sound and says "Try again" or "Almost";
  - "wrong" is never written or said, and no red cross is shown;
  - a sentence the child says is recast: the app repeats it correctly ("Yes! *I am happy.*"), as a good teacher does;
  - after two misses on one item, a hint card explains in Hindi (tracks A–C) and the item comes back later.

## 5. How children learn here (the methods)

| Method | What it means in the course | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Comprehensible input** | Every new word comes with a picture, a sound and its Hindi meaning; stories use known words plus a few new ones | Words, Listen |
| **Total physical response** | Track A and B act out words ("Touch your nose", "Jump!") before saying them | A, B: Learn |
| **Chunks before rules** | Children learn whole useful sentences ("Can I go out, please?") before the grammar behind them | All tracks |
| **Visual grammar** | Tenses and patterns as moving pictures: a timeline for past, now and future; colour-coded sentence parts; tiles that snap into order | Pattern lessons |
| **Retrieval and spacing** | Each item comes back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; an item is "learned" after correct answers on two days a week apart (C230-I) | Warm-up, Review |
| **Shadowing** | Listen to a line, then say it with the voice; hear your own recording side by side | Say |
| **Task-based speaking** | Real situations: shop, classroom, doctor, bus stand, interview, a 1-minute talk | Speak lessons |
| **Hindi as a bridge, fading** | Hindi helps understanding early and steps back as the level rises; "Say it in English" turns a Hindi sentence into English | All; Speak |
| **Low anxiety** | Private practice, recording you can delete, no ranks, no comparison with other children (`09` §6) | Everywhere |
| **Parents in the loop** | Track A is done together; the duet mode (founder #7, `11` E3) lets a grown-up take one part of any role-play; a "talk at home" line after each lesson gives the parent one English sentence to use that evening | All tracks |

## 7. Locks, mastery and review

- **The next lesson opens when this one is done.** Done means: every step seen and the check at 80% or more (track A:
  the grown-up's "we did it"). A lesson may be tried again at once; the check then uses different items where the
  lesson has them.
- **The next unit opens when the unit's review lesson is done.** The day-45 and day-90 assessments open after units 9
  and 18; the next unit does not wait for the assessment's result.
- **Placed units** (from the day-0 check) are open and marked "placed".
- **Spaced review:** every word, pattern sentence and check item a child gets right goes into a review box. It comes
  back after 1, 3, 7 and 21 days; a miss sends it back to day 1. "Learned" means correct on two days at least 7 days
  apart (C230-I). Review needs no lock: the warm-up and the review lesson draw from it.
- **Nothing is lost:** a version update keeps every lesson's key, so progress carries over.

## 8. Progress: the child's view and the parent's report

- **The child sees:**
  - the course map, a winding village path with 18 stops, each stop a unit (open, current, done, locked);
  - stars for lessons done (3 for every check item right on the first try, 2 for 80% or more, 1 for 80% or more after an earlier try; track A gets 3 when the grown-up says it was done);
  - the words collected (a picture word book);
  - the can-do badges.
  - No ranks, no comparison with anyone (`09` §6).
- **The parent sees** (the parent area, behind the PIN):
  - lessons done this week against the weekly goal (a ring);
  - words learned, and learning, as a growing bar by week;
  - patterns learned;
  - the can-do statements achieved;
  - speaking practice minutes by week (a column chart);
  - the listening and check accuracy trend (a line);
  - the day-0, day-45 and day-90 results side by side (paired bars);
  - one suggestion for home ("Ask Meera: *What did you eat today?*").
- **The weekly summary** (`11` R9) adds one English line: the week's lessons, one word to use at home.
- Charts are our own SVG with the chart palette (`design-tokens.ts` `chartSeries`), each with its numbers as labels
  and a table view (`docs/19` §3.5).

## 9. Voice: practice only

- **The course works fully without voice AI.** Every "Say" step has, always:
  - the model sentence in the phone's own voice (labelled "phone's voice"; Indian English where the phone has it);
  - "record and hear yourself": the recording stays on the phone and is deleted when the step closes;
  - a self-check ("I said it").
- **Voice feedback, when switched on in Control:**
  - speech-to-text shows "I heard: …" against the model sentence, word by word;
  - feedback says "Try again" when unsure, never "wrong" (`09` §5);
  - no percentage score until accuracy on Indian accents is measured (`11` E3).
- **Conversation practice, when switched on:** the "Speak" lesson's role-play becomes a short conversation with an
  AI partner. Its script and limits are bound to the lesson; it talks in turns (the child taps to talk); every reply is
  checked before it is spoken; a daily limit applies (5 minutes by default, a Control setting up to 10).
- **Who may use voice AI:**
  - the parent's consent for `voice_practice` (a separate purpose, `03`);
  - a speech provider cleared for minors in code and in Control (`src/lib/ai/adapters/types.ts`);
  - children under 13 only after the provider confirms zero data retention for the account (`01` E18; a second
    switch in Control);
  - no audio is kept by NetCyberAI; a transcript only if the parent turns it on, and the parent can delete it.
- **Offline:** lessons already opened are saved on the phone (the app's offline cache) with their phone-voice audio;
  the check results wait and sync. Voice feedback and conversation say "needs internet".

## 10. Design: colourful, moving, calm

Inside `09`: every motion explains something, `prefers-reduced-motion` turns motion into fades, and the performance
budget holds (≤ 200 KB of JavaScript on first load, no animation library: CSS and SVG only).

- **Track colours** (design tokens only):
  - A: sunshine and tangerine;
  - B: leaf and turquoise;
  - C: royal and cyan;
  - D: grape and violet.
  - Gradients for headers and progress, text at AA contrast (`scripts/check_contrast.py`).
- **The course map:** an SVG village path; stops pop in one after another; the current stop pulses gently; a locked
  stop shows a padlock; a finished unit plants a flower on its stop.
- **Word cards:** a swipeable carousel (scroll-snap); each card flips to show the Hindi meaning; the picture bounces
  once when its sound plays.
- **Pattern visuals:**
  - **the tense timeline:** an arrow from yesterday to tomorrow draws itself; "now" glows; past, present and future
    markers drop onto it as the example sentences play;
  - **sentence builder:** colour-coded tiles (who in royal, doing word in tangerine, what in leaf, when in grape) snap
    into place;
  - **question flip:** "You are happy" turns into "Are you happy?" as the two words swap places;
  - **singular to plural:** one mango becomes three as the -s appears.
- **Listen:** dialogue bubbles appear in turn beside the speakers, the word being spoken lights up (karaoke style), and
  the scene picture sits above.
- **Pictures:** scenes are built from emoji in an SVG frame (a market, a classroom, a field), so no image needs a
  licence and every picture weighs almost nothing. Only emoji from Unicode 12 or earlier are used, so they show on
  older Android phones; the lesson checker refuses others.
- **Celebration:** a short burst of colour when a unit is done and on the assessments' results; a quiet star fill for
  a lesson (`09` §3).
- **A guide character (founder decision, `09` §3):** a talking parrot, "Mitthu", who gives instructions in tracks A–C
  with five states (idle, talking, thinking, happy, try again), drawn in SVG. It is built switchable in Control and
  off until the founder chooses it.

## 11. How the content is made, checked and published

- **Lesson files** live in `data/english/<track>/` (one JSON file per unit with its five lessons) and
  `data/english/<track>/assessments.json`. They are our own writing: no textbook, song or picture is copied.
- **The lesson checker** (`src/lib/english/course.ts`) refuses a file whose lesson misses a step, whose check has
  fewer than 3 items (B–D), whose words lack a Hindi meaning, whose keys clash, whose emoji is not on the list, or
  whose text is above the track's sentence length.
- **Review and publishing:** the files load into the content library as one course version per track, in review
  (as the catalog does, `10` §4). Control → Content shows each lesson as the child will see it; two people approve and
  release, as for syllabus versions. Families read only a released version.
- **Reviewers:** an English teacher for the English, and a Hindi speaker for the Hindi lines, per release (`07` §4).
  Until they have reviewed, the course opens only to staff preview.

## 12. Privacy and safety

- **Consent:** `english_programme` (the course and its progress) and `voice_practice` (voice AI) are separate
  purposes in the consent ledger, given by the parent.
- **Data kept:** the child's lesson results, review boxes, assessment results and voice-practice minutes in the
  `family` schema. No audio. No ranking. Nothing reaches a school unless the family shares it (`05` rule 3).
- **AI sees nothing from the core course:** the lessons are fixed, reviewed content. Only voice practice sends the
  child's words, under §9's gates.
