# Video channels for the Class 5 EVS pilot

From the Claude Code build lead, for Noor and the supervisor. This page proposes the YouTube channels that pilot
children's EVS videos may come from (P1 item 4; C230-R, C230-S, C279). It is a proposal only: no channel is added
anywhere until Noor approves it in Control, and this page changes no setting.

## What Noor does

1. **Approve one channel: NCERT OFFICIAL.** In Control → Content → Videos, under **Channels**, fill in the form:
   - **Channel ID (starts with UC):** `UCT0s92hGjqLX6p7qY9BBrSA`
   - **Channel name:** `NCERT OFFICIAL`
   - **List:** Official
   - Press **Save channel**. It then shows in the list with the "Official" badge.
2. **Check the first picks.** During the first days of the pilot, open the same screen, type `cbse.c5.evs` (the start
   of every Class 5 EVS topic key) in the filter, and press **Reject** on any video that does not fit its topic. A video for the old Class 5 book is the
   most likely miss (see "Things to know").
3. **Add nothing else for now.** Every other channel checked is in "Checked, not recommended" below, with the reason.

Videos only appear once the YouTube key is in Control → AI (C279). That is a separate step and is not part of this page.

## Why only one channel

- Under C230-R, a pilot child sees a video only if its channel is listed in Control as official or approved.
- NCERT, CIET-NCERT, PM eVIDYA and DIKSHA all point to the same YouTube channel, NCERT OFFICIAL. Their websites link
  to no other YouTube channel for primary lessons, and DIKSHA has no YouTube channel of its own that I could find.
- The state channels teach each state's own books, not NCERT's *Our Wondrous World*. They wait until the pilot school's
  board is known (P1 item 6).

## Things to know

- **A very mixed channel.** NCERT OFFICIAL holds about 31,000 videos: lessons, live-class recordings (about 20 to 30
  minutes), audio programmes, sign-language (ISL) programmes, teacher trainings, webinars, events, and Shorts for
  Classes 1 to 3. The video finder keeps only videos whose title (or chapter marks) share words with the topic, and
  ranks videos of 2 to 25 minutes higher, so most of these never reach a child. A reviewer still looks at the picks (step 2 above).
- **The old book is on it too.** Lessons for the old Class 5 book (*Looking Around* / पर्यावरण अध्ययन, for example
  "Ch 14- Jab dharti kaanpi") are still on the channel. A topic with common words, such as water or food, could pick
  one up. Reject it in Control.
- **Hindi will be thin.** For the new book, the Hindi items I found are audio programmes whose titles are in English
  (for example "NCERT Audio Program Grade 5 Our Wonderful World Chapter-2 A River's Journey"). Expect fewer Hindi
  videos than English ones. A topic with no fitting video shows none, and its explanation stands on its own
  (02 §5).
- **What YouTube marks on them.** The three Class 5 videos I opened (`nrThXQTYyZI`, `EckcDQqtNFU`, `8QBwlGjNQzg`) are
  marked family-safe, in the Education category, and allowed to be embedded.
- **Ads, suggestions and comments.** I could not tell from the pages whether NCERT's videos carry ads. The app's player
  already tells the child that YouTube may show ads and suggestions (C279). The player inside the app shows no
  comments; a child who taps through to YouTube sees whatever YouTube shows there.
- **For the supervisor: the licence line in 02 §5.** It says DIKSHA or NCERT videos are not embedded inside the paid
  tier (non-commercial licence: link out instead). The pilot is free, so this does not block it. Before EVS sits behind
  a paid plan, the supervisor decides whether playing NCERT's own YouTube uploads in YouTube's player counts as that.

## How each ID was checked (2026-10-22)

- **The ID, from the channel's own page.** I fetched each channel's YouTube page and read its ID in three places on
  the page: the canonical link (`youtube.com/channel/UC…`), the `externalId` field and the
  `<meta itemprop="identifier">` tag. All three matched for every ID in the two tables. Nothing was typed from memory or
  copied from a search result.
- **The owner, from the official website.** I fetched the official website and found the link to the channel in its
  own page code:
  - ncert.nic.in: the footer's YouTube icon links `youtube.com/channel/UCT0s92hGjqLX6p7qY9BBrSA`, and a banner links
    `youtube.com/@NCERTOFFICIAL/`.
  - ciet.ncert.gov.in: the footer links the same channel ID.
  - ciet.ncert.gov.in/initiative/pmevidya: "Live Telecast" links `youtube.com/c/NCERTOFFICIAL`.
  - CIET's Class 5 PM eVIDYA schedule for August 2025 names the NCERT OFFICIAL YouTube channel for the live classes.
  - pmevidya.education.gov.in links only NIOS's channel (on its CwSN page), and diksha.gov.in's home page links no
    YouTube channel.
- **The Class 5 EVS videos, from the channel's own search page** (searches such as "TWAU Grade-5" and
  "Grade 5 Our Wondrous World").

## How Control uses the list

- Control → Content → Videos → **Channels** saves each channel with its list (Official, Approved or Blocked). Every
  change is written to the audit log. **Remove** takes a channel off the list.
- Each time a topic's videos are refreshed, the finder searches up to 2 listed channels (official ones first) and one
  wider YouTube search. While the wider catalogue is off (C230-R), only videos from listed channels are kept. A short
  list is better, because extra channels share those 2 searches.
- An official channel ranks above an approved one. Blocking a channel rejects all its videos at once.
